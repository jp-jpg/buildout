Plumbing

Liquidity and funding

Net liquidity is the Fed's balance sheet minus the Treasury's cash account and the overnight reverse repo facility: roughly the cash the Fed leaves in the private system. SOFR against the rate the Fed pays on reserves (IORB) says whether that cash is getting scarce. The Fed stopped shrinking its balance sheet on Dec 1, 2025, citing money-market conditions. Federal Reserve

Net liquidity is an accounting identity, not a law of stock prices. Use it for the plumbing (who is draining cash, and how close reserves are to scarce), not to time the index.

Price of protection

Is insurance cheap?

Implied volatility is what an option charges; realized volatility is what the market delivered. The gap is the volatility risk premium: on average buyers of protection pay sellers for carrying crash risk. The desk buys convexity when it is cheap, not when it is needed.

Desk rules: buy convexity when implied sits in the bottom third of its ten-year range and within about two points of its median premium; prefer put spreads and collars when implied is in the top fifth; when the VIX curve inverts, stress is priced now, so monetize in stages instead of buying.

Mechanism

Two kinds of AI selloff

Whether long Treasuries help depends on why the book is falling. Long yields react to the cause of the selloff, and the two causes push them in opposite directions.

Growth scare

Bonds hedge
  1. AI revenue or demand disappoints
  2. Growth and rate expectations fall
  3. Long yields drop
  4. TLT rallies while the book falls

Oct 8, 2026. Reports of weak OpenAI revenue hit AI names; the Nasdaq-100 fell 1.39%. The 30-year yield fell 5.9bp, worth about +0.9% on a bond with TLT's 15.5-year duration.

Financing or inflation shock

Bonds fail
  1. Oil, capex debt or fiscal supply push inflation and issuance up
  2. Term premium rises; the Fed hikes
  3. Long yields climb
  4. TLT falls with the book

Rules

Survival rules the engine enforces

  1. Ruin is the only unrecoverable loss.Size so the 95th-percentile one-year drawdown stays inside the limit, before any view on return. (Taleb's ergodicity point: a bet you cannot repeat forever is not worth its average.)
  2. Barbell the book.Very safe on one side (bills, TIPS), very convex on the other (options, the concentrated AI sleeve). Avoid the fragile middle: leveraged carry that loses everything in one gap.
  3. Prefer contracts to correlations.A put pays by contract. A bond hedge pays only if the correlation holds, and it flipped in 2022 and again in 2026.
  4. Trim on rules.A position that runs 20% over its target weight gets cut back to target. Sell in pieces; never all or nothing.
  5. Know the rate exposure every day.Every name carries a rates beta; the book's total is reported daily next to the hedge ratio.

Rates of change

How fast each signal is moving, and when its rule fires

Level says where a market is. Velocity says where it is going, acceleration says whether it is speeding up, and time to trigger says when the rule fires if the pace holds. A 13-week pace that disagrees with the 4-week pace is a turn in progress.

Velocity, acceleration, time to triggerv13 = least-squares slope of the last 13 weeks of prints, per week. a = v13(now) − v13(13 weeks ago). T = (L* − L) ÷ v weeks, defined only when v points at the line L*. A least-squares slope uses every print in the window, so one bad print moves it far less than a two-point change.

Swans · Fat tails and the hedges that survive them

You cannot predict the tail. You can own it cheaply.

A normal model says a six-sigma month comes once in 84 million years. Since 1871 the S&P 500 has had two. The desk's answer is Taleb's: name the gray swans you can, own convexity for the ones you can't, and never size so that one of them is ruin.

Lab

Does the hedge pay for itself? The compound-growth test

Mark Spitznagel's test: a tail hedge is worth owning only if the book's compound growth is higher with it than without it. A hedge can lose money on average and still raise compound growth, because it shrinks the losses that compounding punishes most.

Compound growthg = exp(∑ ps ln(1 + rs − c + hs)) − 1, where c is the hedge cost and hs its payoff in scenario s.

Capex hits the income statement later, as depreciation. The model below is fitted to each company's reported depreciation, then run forward on its guidance, at the stated server life and at a shorter one.

The modelEach year's capex Cv enters service mid-year; a share s depreciates over the server life Ls, the rest over 25 years. Dt = ∑v Cv [ s·overlap(t, v, Ls)/Ls + (1 − s)·overlap(t, v, 25)/25 ]. s is solved so the model reproduces the reported depreciation in the fit year. Land and construction in progress are not separated, so the wave arrives later than modeled.

      System

      The engine behind the desk.

      Python, about 5,000 lines including tests, run every weekday on GitHub Actions. One timing rule runs through all of it: a value at date t uses data through the close of t, and a decision made at t earns returns from t+1.

      The five stages of the daily run

      1. Data

        FRED curve, credit, volatility, oil, inflation, jobs and financial conditions daily; SEC 10-K and 10-Q facts; 155 years of public data monthly; Databento bars with split and dividend factors; Kalshi odds.

      2. Exposures

        Daily betas to market, AI and rates factors. SPCX leans on TSLA's betas until it has its own history.

      3. Regime

        Stock-bond correlation, exponentially weighted with a 63-day half-life, read with hysteresis: hedging, neutral or amplifying.

      4. Hedge & size

        Min-variance TLT ratio when the regime allows it. Vol target and drawdown cap set gross; Kelly checks the edge.

      5. Prove

        Dollar-exact backtest with costs and financing, deflated Sharpe, and walk-forward forecasts judged on calibration.
